# The 4-week test

**The question:** does an app that adapts to your energy (instead of scolding you) help you keep training?
Not "does the app work" — it does. The only thing left to learn is whether it *matters*.

## Rules of the test
1. **Use only this app** for 4 weeks. Train as you normally would.
2. **Don't add features during the test.** When you think "it should do X", write it in the wishlist below instead.
   Bug fixes are fine. Wishlist items are decided *after* week 4 with evidence.
3. **Decide the success bar now** (below), before you have data. Otherwise you will explain away whatever happens.

## Before you start (5 minutes)
- [ ] Progress tab → **Save backup file** (keep a copy in Drive or email it to yourself).
- [ ] Progress tab → **Clear workout history** to wipe your test workouts, so the recommendations start from real lifts.
- [ ] Set your real **workouts per week** target on the Progress tab.
- [ ] Open Workouts and edit the starter workouts to match what you actually do.
- [ ] Your first session of each lift: pick a weight you can do for the rep range with 1–2 reps spare. The app takes over from session 2.

## Every session
1. Open the app, **tap how you feel honestly** (the whole idea depends on this).
2. Do the workout it gives you. If you're Tired/Exhausted, do *that* version rather than skipping.
3. Log sets as you go. Finish the workout.
4. Once a week: Progress tab → **Save backup file**.

"Workouts done" and "Tired/Exhausted" come from the Progress tab. **Skipped days and ignored suggestions are only
known by you** — the app can't see a workout you never opened, so be honest in the tally.

## Success bar (written before the test — don't move it later)
The concept is **worth continuing** if, over weeks 2–4:
- [ ] You hit **at least 80%** of your weekly target (e.g. 3/week → 9 of 12 sessions or better).
- [ ] On days you felt Tired/Exhausted, you **trained at least half the time** instead of skipping.
- [ ] You followed the suggested weight and reps **at least 80%** of the time without second-guessing it.
- [ ] **At least two lifts** show a rising "estimated best" on the Progress tab.
- [ ] You would be **annoyed if the app disappeared** (be honest).

Mixed results are informative too: 3–4 of 5 = promising but fix what failed; 0–2 = rethink before building more.

## Then: test it on other people (weeks 3–6)
You are one person who built this for yourself, so a good result could just be you. Find **3–5 people** who lift and work
shifts (nurses, warehouse, trades, hospitality, emergency services).
- Send them the link; they install it from their phone's menu ("Add to Home Screen" / "Install app").
- Ask for 2 weeks of normal use. Don't coach them through it — watch where they get stuck.
- After 2 weeks, ask:
  1. How many sessions did you do, and how many would you normally have done?
  2. Did you ever pick Tired/Exhausted? What happened next?
  3. Did you trust the suggested weight? When did you ignore it, and why?
  4. How would you feel if you could no longer use this? (very disappointed / somewhat / not really)
  5. What is the one thing you'd change?
- Their data stays on their phone. If they want to share results, they can screenshot the Progress tab.
- **Strongest signal:** people who keep using it in week 3 without you reminding them. "Nice app" is not a signal.

## Known limits of this version (so you don't mistake them for findings)
- Kilograms only; no pounds setting.
- Your goal (build muscle / get stronger / consistency) is shown but doesn't change recommendations yet.
- Tired days at full weight still don't count towards progression (by design). If this feels unfair in practice, tell me.
- Data lives on one phone; backups are manual. The app nudges you after 14 days.
- It can't see skipped workouts, only the ones you start.
- Progress rules are sensible defaults, not science. If goals feel too easy or too hard, that's useful data — note the exercise and the date.

## After week 4: decide
- **Clear yes** → pick the top 1–2 wishlist items that came up repeatedly, and build only those.
- **Mixed** → fix the specific thing that failed, run 2 more weeks.
- **No** → find out why (tracking effort? motivation? rules?) before touching the code. A "no" is a good result: cheap to learn.
